# Evidence 1 – Governance Blueprint

## 1. Current-State Governance Gap Assessment

GlobalHealth Connect (GHC) has grown through rapid expansion and two acquisitions. 
However, its security governance remains largely ad hoc. The recent contained 
data-leakage near-miss indicates that the current approach needs to become more 
structured and accountable.

| Governance Gap | Current Situation | Business Impact | Priority |

| Unclear security ownership | Security responsibilities are spread across IT, Compliance, HR, Development and business functions. | Important security decisions or risks may not have a clear owner. | High |
| No formal governance forum | There is no established cross-functional Security Steering Committee. | Security disagreements may be handled informally without consistent escalation. | High |
| Inconsistent policies | Policies inherited from acquisitions are not always current or consistent. | Teams may follow different security practices, creating control gaps. | High |
| Reactive risk management | GHC does not currently have a formal proactive security risk assessment process. | Risks may only receive attention after an incident or audit finding. | High |
| Weak business reporting | Technical security metrics are not consistently translated into business impact. | Senior management may find it difficult to prioritise security investment. | Medium |
| Low training engagement | Annual security training is mandatory, but employee engagement is low. | Employees remain exposed to phishing and other human-related security risks. | Medium |

The Director of Information Security Governance reports to executive
management and has direct access to the Board for material security risks.
The Security Steering Committee provides cross-functional governance and
prioritization.
The CTO and IT Operations remain responsible for implementing and operating
technical controls.
Compliance provides regulatory guidance and assurance input.
Business and data owners remain accountable for risks associated with their
services and information.
HR remains responsible for workforce processes such as onboarding and
off boarding.

The RACI model makes the difference between responsibility and accountability
clear. The person or function carrying out an activity is not necessarily the
same person accountable for the final outcome.

4. Governance Rationale

The proposed structure is designed around four main governance principles:

Accountability

Security activities have named owners and defined escalation routes. This
reduces the possibility of security risks being left unresolved because
responsibility is unclear.

Transparency

Security risks, incidents, exceptions and remediation progress should be
reported consistently to management and the Board. Reporting should focus on
material risks and trends rather than excessive technical detail.

Business Alignment

Security should support GHC's strategic objectives rather than operate as an
isolated IT function. Development, IT, Compliance, HR and business owners
therefore participate in governance decisions.

Risk Management

GHC should move from a reactive approach to a structured risk-management
process. Material risks should be identified, assessed, assigned to owners,
treated or formally accepted, and reviewed regularly.

The structure is intended to be proportionate to GHC's size and growth. It
creates formal oversight without introducing unnecessary bureaucracy.
